AR isn't available on this device. You can still rotate and zoom the 3D model.
Stay on the sidewalk. Place the ramp by pointing your phone at the street from the curb. Never step into the street or traffic to use this.

How to use it

  1. Open AR. Tap "View at true scale in AR" on a phone. Android uses Chrome's AR, iPhone and iPad use AR Quick Look.
  2. Let it find the street. Slowly move the phone so it can detect the pavement.
  3. Place it at the curb. The model sits on the street surface. Aim at the street right in front of the curb, where a ramp would meet the street (or at the middle of the curve, for corner designs), and tap to place.
  4. Line it up. Turn it with two fingers until the yellow panel faces the street and the model's curb lines up with the real one. The size is locked to true scale.
  5. Try another design. If it doesn't fit, close AR, pick another design above and place it again.
  6. Look around. Poles, hydrants and signs need 1 ft of clearance from the ramp and landing; drains and utility covers can't sit in them. Take a screenshot to attach to your request.

    Limits

    • This is a rough visual aid, not a design. SDOT engineers pick the ramp type and fit it to each corner. Showing a design that fits can still help your request.
    • Phone AR is usually accurate to within a few percent, but it can drift, and it assumes flat ground. Real streets and sidewalks slope, so the model may float or sink a little.
    • Corners usually get two ramps, one per crossing, so you may want to place one for each.
    • Corner designs are modeled on one curb radius each. Real corners curve more or less, so line up the ramp, not the whole curb.
